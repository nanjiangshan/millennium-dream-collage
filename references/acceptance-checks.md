# Acceptance Checks

## Hard gates

Reject and retry if any answer is no:

1. Is the final composition substantially different from the source layout?
2. Has the intact source photo been removed as the full canvas and background?
3. Is there exactly one clearly identifiable visual protagonist represented by only one or two dominant anchors, with any smaller echoes treated as secondary material?
4. Does every secondary material family stay at four instances or fewer, with no count treated as a quota?
5. Does the protagonist determine the strongest silhouette, direction, rhythm, contrast, or spatial claim without becoming a large uninterrupted field?
6. Is the combined visual center near the middle region, or is an intentional offset clearly stabilized by secondary counterweights?
7. Do secondary elements retain meaningful local independence rather than acting only as decoration around the protagonist?
8. Does every important secondary element relate to at least one element, group, boundary, void, or spatial path?
9. Can the reconstructed whole be described in one sentence as a new abnormal spatial proposition?
10. Is the abnormal relation unmistakable at first glance rather than explainable as normal perspective, ordinary stacking, or a global effect?
11. If the source is highly regular, has at least one governing order—symmetry, continuity, perspective, rhythm, scale, support, or connection—been deliberately broken?
12. If a structure is split, does the split alter at least two relationships among offset, scale, angle, depth, containment, continuation, or reconnection rather than merely add a thin gap?
13. Were repetition, inversion, stretching, rotation, dispersal, recursion, and other strong operations used only when they improve the whole?
14. Do the protagonist and important transformed elements remain recognizable?
15. Does the image read as one composition rather than an inventory, sticker sheet, scrapbook, or collection of equal focal points?
16. Is there functional breathing space without a large dead zone, and has no material family expanded into a continuous mass across most of the canvas?
17. Is collage construction immediately visible through both one or two localized clean boundaries and one altered spatial or structural relationship?
18. Are boundary treatments material-responsive—for example organic silhouettes for trees and localized geometric seams for architectural or scenic planes?
19. Is the result free from torn-paper edges, kraft paper, yellowed paper, scrapbook texture, global fading, warm vintage grading, and full-image grain?
20. Is the result free from unrequested pixelation, color inversion, external objects, decorative text, and complex UI?
21. If a spatial engine is present, does it organize depth or motion without replacing the intended protagonist?
22. Would removing any prominent anomaly make the image stronger? If yes, remove that anomaly before accepting.

## Reject these near misses

- the original photograph with a filter or light rearrangement;
- one centered subject surrounded by decorative objects that do not interact;
- extracted objects displayed in separate zones like a specimen or mood board;
- a scrapbook or torn-paper montage;
- several equal-sized subjects with no clear protagonist;
- more than two dominant protagonist anchors or more than four instances of one secondary family;
- a protagonist that is large but does not control the composition;
- secondary material that has no local relationship or independent purpose;
- every secondary element mechanically pointing toward the protagonist;
- repetition used only because the source contains similar forms;
- a mandatory large-and-small duplicate of the protagonist;
- inversion, stretch, rotation, or scatter used as a visible trick rather than a structural decision;
- a corridor, stair, bridge, road, or wall extended until it becomes the entire image and demotes the intended protagonist;
- a clean but faithful reconstruction of the original scene;
- a plausible architectural enlargement or tidy cascade whose order remains normal;
- one material family growing into a large continuous block that fills most of the image;
- an overly full composition with no useful breathing space;
- a seamless reconstruction with no clean visible crop edge or white seam;
- every material, including trees and foliage, forced into similar tilted rectangles or outlined panels;
- a composition pushed into one corner while the remaining canvas becomes a dead empty zone;
- a building split only by a thin white stripe while both halves retain the same alignment, scale, angle, and depth;
- excessive gaps and fragmentation that destroy the overall rhythm;
- a cozy, nostalgic, handmade, or uniformly vintage result with weak uncanny tension;
- visual aging used as a substitute for millennium or dreamlike feeling.

## Focused retry map

- **No clear protagonist:** strengthen its silhouette, contrast, placement, directional control, or uniqueness; simplify competing focal points.
- **Visual center is misplaced:** move the combined cluster toward the middle or add smaller counterweights that restore balance without creating another focal point.
- **Too many elements:** reduce the protagonist to one or two instances, reduce every secondary family to four or fewer, and remove any instance that adds density without a new relationship.
- **Secondaries feel decorative:** give them a relationship, counter-role, subplot, spatial function, or remove them.
- **Everything points to the protagonist:** let one secondary group form a counterpoint, interruption, or independent micro-scene while preserving hierarchy.
- **Looks like an inventory or sticker sheet:** connect groups through overlap, enclosure, continuation, emergence, obstruction, shared direction, or a spatial engine.
- **Looks too fragmented:** reduce gaps, interlock major pieces, consolidate the silhouette, and restore one overall path.
- **Looks too normal:** change one spatial relationship—scale, continuity, connection, depth, repetition, looping, suspension, or orientation—without adding unrelated effects.
- **Regular source stayed regular:** name its governing order and break one part through asymmetry, scale discontinuity, tilted containment, interrupted rhythm, or impossible reconnection.
- **Anomaly feels decorative:** remove it or rebuild it as part of the main proposition.
- **Repetition feels mechanical:** reduce or omit it, or change its role to depth, growth, looping, or emphasis.
- **Spatial engine takes over:** lower its contrast or detail, crop or conceal it, and restore protagonist dominance.
- **Looks like the source:** replace the original spatial order and rebuild relationships around the proposition.
- **Looks like scrapbook:** remove paper texture, torn edges, isolated labels, and equal gaps; return to clean source-derived forms.
- **Looks seamless instead of collaged:** introduce one clean white seam, crisp crop boundary, or tilted image edge at a meaningful structural joint; do not tear or scatter the image.
- **Every element looks panelized:** keep geometric seams only on selected architecture or spatial planes; restore organic silhouettes and masking for trees, foliage, crowds, and other living forms.
- **Split is too timid:** change at least two structural relationships—offset, scale, angle, depth, containment, continuation, or reconnection—while keeping the intervention localized.
- **Composition is too full:** remove repeated forms and continuous masses, consolidate the active cluster, and restore functional breathing space.
- **Composition is too empty:** enlarge or recenter the active relationship, or add a small secondary echo that strengthens balance; do not fill the void with unrelated material.
- **Color looks filtered:** restore source color and clarity; use a clean quiet base field without global grading.

## Final viewing test

Ask three questions:

1. What is remembered first?
2. What relationships remain interesting after the protagonist is noticed?
3. What impossible new whole has been created?

The result passes when these answers identify one protagonist, meaningful secondary relationships, and one coherent uncanny proposition.
