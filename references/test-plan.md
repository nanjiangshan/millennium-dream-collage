# Test Plan

This version tests source selection, hierarchy, relational reconstruction, spatial tension, and composition. It does not test pixelation, color inversion, external decorative assets, text, legacy UI, or global millennium styling.

## Test set

Use owned or permitted photos covering:

- dense repeated architecture or products;
- one strong object or vehicle;
- stairs, corridors, roads, bridges, or other spatial engines;
- interiors with several small details;
- landscapes or cityscapes;
- people, crowds, animals, or relationship scenes;
- sources where repetition is useful and sources where it should be omitted.

## Record the private decisions

For each result, record:

- visual protagonist and the basis of its dominance;
- dominant protagonist-anchor count, smaller motif echoes, and per-secondary-family counts;
- independently meaningful secondary material;
- relationship network among important elements;
- spatial engine or reason to omit one;
- one-sentence reconstructed proposition;
- the source's governing order and the order deliberately broken;
- mass distribution and movement path;
- optional abnormal operations and why each improves the result;
- repetition decision and why it was used or omitted;
- background and visual-treatment decision.
- visible clean collage boundary and altered relationship;
- visual-center balance, breathing space, and density decision.
- material-responsive boundary treatment;
- if a structure is split, the offset, scale, angle, depth, containment, continuation, or reconnection changes that make it decisive.

## Score 0–2

- departure from the source layout;
- clarity of the single protagonist;
- compliance with the one-to-two protagonist and four-per-secondary-family ceilings;
- useful independence of secondary material;
- strength of relationships among elements;
- coherence of the reconstructed whole;
- uncanny spatial tension;
- clarity of the broken source order;
- suitability of selected operations;
- recognition after transformation;
- control of negative space;
- balance of the combined visual center;
- resistance to large continuous material fields;
- visibility of clean collage construction without torn-paper treatment;
- material-responsive treatment of architecture, scenes, organic material, and linear details;
- boldness of any selected split or dislocation;
- independence from a fixed template;
- absence of scrapbook, torn-paper, kraft, and global vintage treatment;
- absence of unrequested effects;
- resistance to inventory and sticker-sheet layouts;
- resistance to spatial-engine monopoly.

## Failure conditions across the set

Reject the revision if outputs repeatedly:

- default to inversion, repetition, stretching, or another named operation regardless of source;
- repeat the protagonist in a mandatory large-and-small pattern;
- place isolated secondary objects around one central subject;
- force every secondary element to point toward the protagonist;
- become torn-paper, kraft-paper, scrapbook, cozy vintage, or globally filtered images;
- break successful wholes into separate fragments to prove collage;
- restore the original scene too faithfully;
- turn a regular source into a tidy, plausible enlargement or cascade;
- allow one material family to expand into a large continuous mass covering most of the image;
- leave no useful breathing space or push the entire composition into a corner beside a dead empty zone;
- hide all crop seams and rebuild a seamless rendered environment;
- exceed one or two dominant protagonist anchors or four instances of one secondary family;
- force trees, foliage, people, and other organic material into the same rectangular panel treatment as architectural planes;
- use a thin white gap as the only evidence of a supposedly bold structural split;
- lose a clear protagonist;
- let corridors, stairs, roads, bridges, or other spatial engines replace the intended protagonist;
- use the same mass distribution and movement combination without source-based justification.

## Comparison method

When revising after a failed result, change only the rule responsible for the failure. Compare the new result with the previous one on hierarchy, central balance, scale variety, relationships, wholeness, density, material-responsive boundaries, visible collage construction, broken source order, uncanny tension, and visual treatment. Do not accumulate a universal rule from one example unless the same failure appears across different source types.
